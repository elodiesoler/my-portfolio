About me

Started with a pencil.
Still haven't stopped.

From field research to shipped software, I enjoy turning operational complexity into products people rely on.

I’ve been drawing and making things since I was seven.

Today, those sketches become software that helps installers, field teams and robots work together. I’ve learned that products are defined not by how they look, but by what they enable in the real world.

Working closely with engineers, installers and commercial teams reshaped how I think about product design. Every decision carries consequences beyond usability - it affects delivery, cost and long-term adoption. Good ideas only matter if they hold up when they meet operational reality.

That’s why my approach is grounded in outcomes. I connect design decisions to measurable impact, prototype early using code and AI-assisted development and validate before they consume engineering time.

I’m most interested in where software becomes infrastructure: the unglamorous environments where climate progress actually gets built: under floors, on scaffolding and in installers’ vans.

Sketching out loud

Thinking on paper

Whatever the project, sketching remains my go-to for early brainstorming. It gives stakeholders something loose enough to react to, before anyone gets attached to high-fidelity designs. With AI accelerating the path to resolution, that flexibility matters more and more.

I also illustrate my reflections on the quieter truths of working in product and software development, things many people notice but few say out loud.
