Context

Usability under real operational constraints

After six generations of incremental changes, Q-Bot's robot control interface had become increasingly difficult to evolve. A platform migration provided the opportunity to rethink the experience from the ground up.

The challenge was to design for installers operating robots in confined spaces with limited visibility, where every interaction affects speed, accuracy, and confidence. I led the redesign end-to-end, partnering with engineers and field operators to create a system that is still used today across 50+ robots in the UK and France.

Challenge

Friction in critical workflows

Operators were already working in a challenging physical environment. The interface compounded that challenge with fragmented workflows, limited visibility, and unnecessary interactions, making critical tasks slower and more error-prone.

Process

Shadowed real installs

Interviews and on-site observations showed that operators had to adapt to the software, rather than the software supporting the way they worked.

Mapping the critical tasks

I broke down the most frequent operator tasks to understand where time and effort were being lost. The friction came less from individual screens and more from moving between them: extra steps, device switching and divided attention.

Designing within ROS

Working end-to-end meant designing within the constraints of the robotics platform.

For instance, we explored displaying foam thickness as a live overlay on the camera feed, but building and maintaining it within ROS would have required significant effort for uncertain value. Instead, I designed a simple thickness readout in the corner of the screen. In field use, it provided the information operators needed at a glance, without the complexity of a full visual overlay.

Solution

A focused, scalable interface

I designed a more focused and scalable interface that:

  • Maximises the primary camera feed to improve real-time visibility
  • Surfaces essential controls directly within the main workflow
  • Enables navigation via a single input device (gamepad-inspired interaction), reducing friction
  • Simplifies information architecture to support faster decision-making
  • Introduces clearer system feedback (e.g. startup status) to improve operator confidence

The solution was developed iteratively, balancing user needs with the technical constraints of the robotics system (ROS).

The key design move

Maximising the camera feed and moving to a single gamepad-inspired input, cutting interaction cost exactly where visibility and speed matter most.
